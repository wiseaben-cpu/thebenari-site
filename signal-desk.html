Built it: LLM agents on a live broker

In the first version, LLM agents produced the signals and placed the orders on a live brokerage account through an MCP tool server. It won 62% of its trades.

A win rate counts how often a position closed up. It says nothing about whether the signal caused it. The market rose 20% over the same window, and in a rising market most long positions win.

Tested it: the win rate was bull-market beta

The measure that matters is the information coefficient — how well a signal's forecast correlates with the return that actually follows. Out of sample the AI signals scored an IC of 0.006 at a t-stat of 0.4. That is indistinguishable from zero.

The equity curve says the same thing. Over the window the strategy returned +18% while the S&P 500 returned +20%, so it finished two points behind the index it was trading inside. The 62% win rate was bull-market beta, not skill.

So I retired it.

Rebuilt it: momentum that holds out of sample

The rebuild throws out the LLM signal and ranks on momentum instead. Out of sample it scores an IC of 0.13 at a t-stat of 8.2, and the curve ends at +115% against the same +20% market — 95 points of excess. The toggle above the chart switches between the two versions.

Both are tested the same way, so the comparison is fair: walk-forward, net of 35 bps, over a 24-month window.

How to read these numbers

Information coefficient is the correlation between a signal's forecast and the return that follows it; zero means the signal knows nothing. The t-stat says how likely an IC that size is to be chance — 0.4 is chance, 8.2 is not. Walk-forward means the model is fit on earlier data and scored only on the stretch that comes after, so it is never graded on data it has already seen. Net of 35 bps means trading costs are subtracted before any of these figures are computed.

The series shown here are illustrative sample data, not realized returns. Nothing on this page is investment advice or a recommendation. The production system is a private repo.
