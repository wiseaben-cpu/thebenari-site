BenAri 02 — Tested to break

It traded its own best worst ideas

An autonomous system that screens, sizes and places real orders on a live Robinhood account, unattended. It ran real money for 37 trading days and lost 5.0% while the S&P 500 rose 1.4%. The loss isn't the interesting part. The picks it chose to trade did far worse than the ones it passed over — and the more confident it was, the worse they were.

Built it: an unattended book on a live broker

The system scores candidates, decides what to hold, sizes each position to real buying power, and places the orders itself on a Robinhood account. No human in the loop. Over the window it made 103 screener runs, scored 337 candidates, produced 233 recommendations and placed 43 orders, of which 32 filled.

The code froze on 22 June. Everything after that date is one unchanged build running on its own — which is what makes the record worth reading at all. Nothing was tuned mid-flight to flatter it.

Tested it: it bought its own worst ideas

Every recommendation is tracked, whether or not it was traded — which makes the system gradeable against itself. Of 233 recommendations, it chose to trade 132 and passed over 101. Held to today, the ones it traded are down 24.7%. The ones it passed over are down 1.0%.

That gap is not a quirk of timing or sizing. It holds on every single entry date and at every conviction level, and it runs the wrong way: the picks it marked high conviction and traded are the worst group on the page, down 31.3%. Its confidence was inversely related to its results.

The cause is concentration. Those 233 recommendations cover only 25 distinct companies, and just 10 were ever traded — dominated by two names it kept re-entering, RKLB (49 recommendations) and ASTS (39). Its own memory layer eventually wrote the lesson down: "Hard skip on RKLB/product_launch, no exceptions." By then the money was spent.

One thing did work. Those traded picks would have lost 24.7% held to today; the account only lost 4.8%, because the stops kept firing and cutting them. The risk layer is the reason this is a bad quarter instead of a wipeout — the selection logic is what failed.

Caught it: the ledger was wrong by $47.61

The app keeps its own ledger of what it thinks it owns. Reconciling that ledger against the broker's actual fills found it disagreed in 12 places: exits recorded at the wrong price, three trades the broker executed that the ledger never recorded, and three positions the ledger booked as closed that Robinhood still holds.

Its own books claimed a realized loss of $91.46. The broker says $43.86. The ledger was overstating losses by $47.61 — more than the real loss itself.

Every figure on this page comes from the broker, not the app, for exactly that reason. A system that reports its own performance and grades its own homework will eventually tell you a comfortable lie. The reconciliation is the part I would build first next time.

What this can't tell you

Thirty-seven trading days, 15 completed round trips, 10 traded names, one $1,500 account. That is far too small to separate a bad strategy from bad luck, and nothing here should be read as evidence that momentum, or LLM agents, or this system, does or doesn't work. A result this size would need to repeat across many more trades and a full market cycle before it meant anything.

What it does show is that the machinery works end to end: it screens, decides, sizes, places real orders, tracks what it passed over, and can be caught lying to itself. That was the thing being tested.

Method

Positions, fills, cash and realized P&L are pulled from the broker; the equity curve is rebuilt by replaying every fill in FIFO order and marking holdings at daily closes. Benchmark is SPY buy-and-hold over the identical window. Round trips are FIFO-matched broker fills, cross-checked against Robinhood's own realized-P&L figure. Recommendation returns are marked from the recommended entry price to the latest close, so they show the pick held rather than the trade taken. Commission-free broker; prices are unadjusted daily closes.

Real money, live orders, 9 June – 31 July 2026. Past results say nothing about future ones. Nothing on this page is investment advice or a recommendation, and it is a record of one small personal account, not an offer to manage anyone else's. The production system is a private repo.
